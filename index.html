What's IronWood?

IronWood is a crime thriller by Michael Connelly and the second novel in his Catalina series. It's published in the United States by Little, Brown and Company on May 19, 2026. The story follows Detective Sergeant Stilwell, an officer of the Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department assigned to Catalina Island. The story builds on the setting and character established in Nightshade, the first in the series, while turning up the investigative challenges facing Stilwell.

It all begins to unfold when Stilwell and his deputies answer a tip from a confidential informant about a mysterious nighttime landing of a plane at a remote island airstrip. The drug drop goes bad, shots are fired and the plane escapes. Stilwell is then sidelined by an internal investigation as the department investigates the failed operation. But he's determined to find out what happened and his own investigation unearths a potential connection between an unclaimed backpack and a woman who disappeared years ago while on a hike.

The investigation ultimately takes Stilwell and Detective Renée Ballard of the Los Angeles Police Department’s Open-Unsolved Unit. Their work covers the channel between Catalina Island and the mainland and it opens up a bigger criminal mystery. The novel thus combines police procedural elements with missing-person investigations and institutional conflict. Readers searching for IronWood US are often searching for this American edition, though the story is also available in other English-language markets.

How Does IronWood Work?

IronWood is a novel that depends on a process of investigation to build its mystery, not a product formula or practical procedure. Connelly constructs an apparently criminal event, then tracks its aftermath through departmental eyes and slowly weaves disparate evidence together. The discovery of the backpack gives Stilwell another lead to follow, expanding the scope from a botched drug run to a more complicated story.

The story bounces between the immediate problems of policing Catalina Island and the larger investigation involving Ballard. The structure connects the island’s somewhat isolated setting to the resources and open cases of the Los Angeles Police Department. Readers of Connelly’s interconnected crime fiction will be familiar with Ballard and may appreciate that her appearance connects the Catalina series to his broader fictional universe.

Benefits of IronWood

One of the great strengths of IronWood is its location. Catalina Island is a unique setting for a police thriller. The beautiful setting of the island is set against organised crime, mysterious disappearances and bureaucratic pressure. This contrast lends itself to suspense, giving the investigation a more localised feel than a typical big-city detective story. But those who enjoy urban crime fiction may find the island setting to be less familiar or less appealing.

Another possible plus is the portrayal of Detective Sergeant Stilwell. In addition to the central mystery, his professional problems add to the tension of the story, especially when the constraints of his department impede his efforts to investigate. His doggedness lends a certain investigative focus to the story, though readers may not be convinced by his willingness to keep following leads, even if it could damage his career even more.

The arrival of Renée Ballard adds another dimension to the story. Her ties to the LAPD's Open-Unsolved Unit allow the investigation to go beyond the island, while also bringing in a familiar face to those who have read Connelly's other novels. However, readers who aren't already familiar with Ballard's past appearances may miss some of the larger picture connections between the author's different series.

Nightshade, published in 2025, brings in Detective Sergeant Stilwell and sets the series on Catalina Island. IronWood continues that storyline in a second investigation, making the first book the more natural choice for readers who care about character development in chronological order.

Another choice is The Waiting, with Detective Renée Ballard, arriving in 2024. For those interested in Ballard's investigative work, her series might be a good read before or after IronWood. The two books provide different perspectives, but the link between Ballard and Stilwell makes them appropriate for readers interested in Connelly’s interconnected crime fiction. Another choice is the long-running Harry Bosch series, with a sizeable back catalogue.

IronWood has the advantage of a continuing protagonist and larger fictional universe over a standalone mystery, but continuity needs more care. If you want a story that is completely stand-alone, you may prefer a Connelly stand-alone novel.
